Futhark is a data-parallel functional array language focused on good
run-time performance, with a particular focus on highly restricted
devices such as GPUs.  As a result, Futhark in its initial design was
extremely limited: No recursion, no polymorphism, no sum types, no
higher-order functions, and no bounds checking.  After years of work,
most of these restrictions have been lifted, giving Futhark most of the
capabilities of a 70s ML.  In this talk, I will discuss why these
features were problematic to begin with, how we addressed them, and why
Futhark still doesn't support general recursion.
