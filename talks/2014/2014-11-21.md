Lenses (bidirectional programs) are usually represented as pairs of functions,
going forwards and backwards respectively. The functions in such a pair have to
be consistent with each other to make sense, and the technique of
correctness-by-construction is commonly employed to program lenses from more
primitive ones. The cost of such an approach is the specialized combinators
that need to be designed by language implementers and used by programmers,
resulting in an inconvenient programming style. We advocate bidirectional
programming in the style of normal unidirectional programming, and will show
how it can be done.
