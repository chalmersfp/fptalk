title: "Blink: wireless programming for hardware dummies"

Software-defined radio (SDR) brings the flexibility of software to the domain
of wireless protocol design, promising both an ideal platform for research and
innovation and the rapid deployment of new protocols on existing hardware.
However, existing SDR platforms either require careful hand-tuning of low-level
code, or are too slow to be useful in the real world.  In this talk we will
present Blink, a software-defined radio platform that is both easily
programmable and performant. Blink introduces a novel programming model
tailored to wireless physical layer tasks and captures the inherent and
important distinction between data and control paths in this domain. We
describe an optimizing compiler for Blink, provide a detailed evaluation, and
give a line-rate Blink implementation of 802.11a/g.

This is joint work with Bozidar Radunovic (MSRC), Gordon Stewart (PhD student
at Princeton), Mahanth Gowda (PhD student at UIUC), and Geoffrey Mainland
(Drexel).
