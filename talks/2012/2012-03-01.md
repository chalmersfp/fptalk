speaker: "Gordon Pace (University of Malta)"
title: "Compensations in Runtime Verification"
place: "3364"
date: 2012-03-01 13:15:00
---
Typically systems are monitoring either synchronously - blocking the
system until the necessary analysis is done to confirm that the
computation is correct, or asynchronously - in which the actions of
the system are logged, and concurrently verified to be correct. The
former guarantees that the system does not overrun states in which an
error is detected, but at the cost of substantial system overhead. The
latter reduces the cost of monitoring to that of logging the events,
but may lead to situations where the system was allowed to proceed
well beyond a state in which an error occurred. We are currently
looked at ways in which one can address this drawback using the notion
of compensations. Compensations have long been used in long-lived
transactions, in which it is undesirable to lock resources due to
their expected duration. The solution typically adopted to allow for
the processes to proceed despite that they may potentially perform
actions which would later have to be undone, or compensated
for. Compensations provide a design pattern to address these
issues. In this talk, I will be outlining the notions of compensation
and their use in runtime verification. I plan to dedicate the last
part of the talk to a discussion of compensations from a monadic
perspective.
