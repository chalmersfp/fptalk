speaker: "Emil Axelsson"
title: "Lightweight higher-order rewriting in Haskell"
place: "3364"

Algebraic data types are an excellent tool for representing abstract syntax
trees. In particular, syntactic rewrite rules can be conveniently expressed by
pattern matching directly on the structure of terms. However, when working with
ASTs that contain variable binders, structural matching is usually too
low-level, requiring us to deal with variable names and scopes explicitly. In
contrast, *higher-order rewriting* (HOR) is a technique where lambda
abstractions are allowed on the left-hand side of rules. In this setting,
pattern matching is done up to the equivalences of the lambda calculus, thus
relieving us from the burden of dealing with explicit variable names. As a
result, HOR rules are also guaranteed to be well-scoped.

I will present a generic library for a restricted kind of higher-order
rewriting in Haskell. As common in embedded DSLs, the rules are expressed using
smart constructors and the binding facilities of the host language, making them
very concise and readable. By statically imposing certain restrictions on
patterns, the rules can be executed efficiently; yet, the rules are expressive
enough to capture some interesting simplifications of programs in the Feldspar
EDSL.

This is very much work in progress. The internals of the library are not yet
fully implemented, but hopefully will be before the talk.
