When we ask today's state-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) whether an object is to the left or right, they often get the answer correctly. But does a correct answer mean the model truly understands the physical 3D scene? Or is it merely exploiting language shortcuts, making lucky guesses based on text common sense?   

In this talk, I will introduce our ECCV 2026 work, **CRISP**. Moving beyond conventional black-box Q&A benchmarks that reward blind guessing, CRISP forces models to "draw" their internal 3D spatial beliefs as structured metric 3D Scene Graphs. By programmatically checking whether a model's textual answers agree with its perceived physical geometry, we open up the black box to diagnose visual spatial intelligence.   

Our findings uncover several surprising phenomena in frontier AI:   
- **The Illusion of Correctness**: Models frequently guess the right answer while hallucinating completely backward 3D distances and depths.   
- **The Perception-Reasoning Disconnect**: Models like Gemini can accurately perceive 3D layouts, yet completely ignore them during logical reasoning.   
- **The "Thinking" Trap**: Counter-intuitively, scaling test-time compute (extended thinking) does not fix metric blindness. It often amplifies linguistic rationalization, allowing self-generated text to override visual reality.
