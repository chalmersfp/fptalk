Clinical risk assessment tools are calculators developed from well defined patient cohorts to model the associative risk of an observed outcome. These tools span a broad range of clinical practice and outcome severity, all depending on the viewed need from the clinicians. The utility of the tools stems from their performance in predicting the desired outcome, their cohort restrictions and their conformance to using variables and formatting aligning with routine practice. This talk will go into more detail regarding the landscape of clinical tool utility with focus on two projects related to the topic.

Firstly, we developed a pipeline using medical toolcalling to generate decision-focused tool-grounded clinical assessments (DTG-CAs) as a basis to predict in-ICU mortality either via zero-shot prompting of an LLM or a fine-tuned language encoder. Our framework integrates domain-relevant tools into LLM reasoning to generate DTG-CAs, enabling the model to incorporate additional domain knowledge in reasoning when generating the assessments. Secondly, we work on developing a framework to increase the utility of clinical tool calling by dispatching and repairing problematic clinical tool calls with LLMs based on recognized electronic health record data quality criteria.
