speaker: "Johan Nordlander"
title: "Strict and pure functional programming in a world of message-passing objects -- research issues related to the Timber language experiment"
place: "EB"
date: 2010-10-15 10:15:00
---
Timber is a programming language that claims to be purely functional, classically object-oriented and inherently concurrent at the same time; while fostering a reactive model of interaction that naturally lets its real-time behavior be controlled by declaration. This mix of facets does not imply an overwhelming complexity, though -- Timber notably rather lacks many complicating features of contemporary languages, such as reference cells, synchronization operations, blocking i/o and global system calls. Compared to Haskell in particular, Timber can be thought of as a strict syntactic cousin, whose IO top-level is replaced by a monad of communicating processes reminiscent of Erlang.

In this talk I will present a brief overview of Timber and the goals that motivate the language, before I move on to describe four current research issues that emanate from the attempt to embed purely functional programming within an object-oriented context:

- Defining a formal semantics that captures real-time correctness of concurrent execution.
- Combining nominal subtyping with qualified types and type inference.
- Extracting underlying static structures from highly parameterized programs for analysis purposes.
- Compiling Timber efficiently to C as well as Javascript.

Time will not permit an in-depth coverage of this mixture of topics, of course, but I include them all with the primary aim to properly present myself and my research interests. With some luck, there will be room for a more focused follow-up talk at a later date.
