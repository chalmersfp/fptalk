A well known weakness of QuickCheck-style property based testing
is the lack of feedback when a property can not be falsified. When
QuickCheck says "OK, passed 100 tests" it can mean anything from
"I have done nothing useful" to "there are almost certainly no bugs
here".

Mutation testing offers a way of estimating where in this spectrum a
test suite is. By making small random mutations to the tested code
(thus simulating bugs) we estimate how likely a bug is to be detected
by the tests.

But the success of QuickCheck is largely based on how lightweight it
is, and particularly that it is black box. Mutation testing in the
traditional sense is inherently white box. This makes the tools
complicated to write and brittle in the presence of language extensions,
FFI, meta-programming etc.

I present some sketches for what is possibly the worlds first black box
mutation testing framework. I get some encouraging results when it
comes to evaluating the quality of properties, and the core of the
framework is truly tiny (in the area of 10 lines of code).
