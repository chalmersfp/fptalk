Nick Frolov's master thesis in IESD program is
dedicated to improvement of FlexSoC toolchain and exploration of FlexSoC
as platform for functional programs.

FlexSoC was originally conceived as a project to assess how run-time
flexibility of processor datapath can improve performance and power
dissipation. Although the hardware has matured, the compiler is falling
behind, and therefore many research opportunities cannot be explored.
E.g. it is hard to experiment with new blocks (accelerators or other
execution units) or partially connected datapath since necessary changes
on the compiler side are non-trivial.

Stages of a compiler back-end perform transforms between several
internal representations, and transforms are largely defined by
functionality and constraints of a given variant of hardware. An
approach to managing hardware variability is to design a higher-order
library for implementing required code generators. A compiler for
existing FlexSoC variant, FlexCore, could be trivially reimplemented
with help of such a library. A platform description that defines a
compiler could actually be viewed as a program written in a DSL.
